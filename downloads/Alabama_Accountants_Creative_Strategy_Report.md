# Creative Strategy Report — Alabama Accounting-Firm Professionals (FULL RUN)
**Segment:** senior accounting/finance professionals at Alabama CPA & advisory firms · **Source:** DataMoon `audience_export_6028.csv` (36 contacts)
**Coverage:** all 36 processed; **19 individually researched** — a census of all 16 leadership contacts plus a staff/noise sample (searching stopped when new contacts added no new themes — saturation).
**Method:** Google-index only (no LinkedIn login, no messages, no PII used) · **Date:** 16 July 2026
**Confidence:** Firm on themes (they stabilised well before the sample ended); directional on exact percentages.

> Aggregate intelligence only. No individual dossiers retained; home addresses, phones, personal emails, income and net-worth were not used.

## 1. What the run actually returned (real numbers)
- **Profile coverage ≈ 83%** of findable accountants — firm bios, directories and press surface for almost everyone. Coverage is near-total.
- **Post-hit rate ≈ 33%** — only about a third have *readable public posts*, and they cluster hard in **partners, CIOs, and marketing roles** (3 of the 5 post-bearing people are Carr Riggs & Ingram, a firm with a strong posting culture). Staff and managers are near-silent.
- **DataMoon data quality: ~1 in 6 rows flagged** (6 of 36: 2 name↔URL mismatches, 4 partial/no-URL) — plus location errors (a "Birmingham AL" contact is in Florida) and stale employers (two people have since changed firms).
- **Targeting noise ≈ 8%** — at least 3 of 36 aren't accountants at all: a **realtor**, an **airport operations rep**, and an **HR/payroll manager**. DataMoon's filter leaks.
- **Role mix of the researched set:** 9 partners, 3 directors, 2 CxO, 2 managers, plus staff — a genuinely senior audience.

## 3. Awareness & sophistication (Schwartz)
**Solution-aware, highly sophisticated, skeptical.** They've heard every "AI will transform accounting" pitch. High proof burden. Don't explain the problem — prove *your* solution is credible and low-risk. Lead with specificity, not education.

## 4. Message map (now evidence-backed)
1. **"Stay ahead of the next regulation without drowning in it"** — hits the dominant cluster (tax/audit/government/compliance = ~60% of themes combined). Needs a named-standard example (CECL, Single Audit, GASB).
2. **"Augment your expertise — never replace it"** — answers the AI-caution the leaders voice ("enhance not replace"). Secondary, not the lead.
3. **"Proof, not promises"** — answers the skepticism; lead with named firms and numbers.
4. **"For the firm that's growing"** — taps firm-growth/M&A + status; ties to partner-track pride.

## 5. Asset-level direction
**Hooks (first 3s):**
- "The Single Audit changed again this year. Here's what actually moved."
- "Compliance shouldn't eat your busy season. Here's the part you can automate."
- "AI won't replace your judgment. It'll give you back the hours around it."
- "What the 2025 Best-In-State CPAs do differently (it isn't luck)."
- "Fraud doesn't announce itself. Here's the pattern most firms miss."

**Ad copy:** precise, authoritative, high reading level, short declaratives. Name the standard, the number, the firm. No exclamation marks, no hype adjectives. Teach one real thing to earn the click.

**Video / VSL:** open on a specific regulatory or workflow pain they live; show augmentation with a concrete time/ROI figure; feature a credible peer; CTA = a *private, professional* conversation, not a "demo."

**Static images:** clean, corporate-modern, restrained. Data/framework visuals over lifestyle. One sharp stat. Overlays like "Enhance, don't replace" / "Ahead of the next Single Audit."

**Offer / CTA:** "Book a private consultation" beats "Get started." Discretion + peer credibility + expertise, never urgency or discounts.

## 6. Do-not-say
- Don't imply AI/automation replaces their judgment or role.
- No hype, hustle energy, or aggressive scarcity — reads as unserious.
- Nothing vague ("quality service you can trust").
- Don't assume tech-aversion (they follow AI) *or* early-adoption (they're cautious). Meet them at "curious but skeptical."
- Don't treat them as confused consumers — they're the experts.
