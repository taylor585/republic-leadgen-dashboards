## 1. Strategic context — what actually changed

The centre of gravity is moving **from the full AI-agency consultancy offer to the Republic lead gen offer.**

- **Republic lead gen offer** = a ~$20k *done-with-you* (≈90% done-for-you) investment. The client becomes the CEO of their own pay-per-lead business, powered by Taylor's **LeadHunter** system plus a stack of supporting tools. Designed so AI handles most of the fulfilment, targeting **500–1,000 clients** at scale.
- **AI agency / consultancy offer** = still sold, but **internally only** now (the outside sales team won't sell it unless it comes in through the lead gen funnel as an upsell). Because it's sold internally we keep a higher margin, so **pricing drops across the board** (holding ~$15k as a discount lever / "in the back pocket").
- The lead gen offer is effectively a **subset of the full agency offer**, which is why sales reps can upsell lead gen buyers into the full consultancy.
- Emphasis goes where **AI can carry the fulfilment load the most** — that is the lead gen offer.

**Why the urgency:** Ryan & Cole start selling within ~a week. History (18 deals in one week at a $50k price point in March, fulfilment backed up for two months) means we must expect a demand spike and design fulfilment/marketing so Taylor is **never the bottleneck.**

## 3. The model — engines, funnels, and the lead layers

### Two supply engines
- **Engine 1 — LeadHunter (scraping / behavioural intent mining):** Taylor's system. Finds people *discussing the problem we solve* on online platforms — recent, high-intent signals (e.g. someone literally talking about wanting lead gen). 0–100 intent scoring, ~91% match rate. **No ad spend required.** This is the primary buyer-acquisition engine early on. Self-learning: it tests searches, records what returns 80+ quality, and rebuilds its database around what works.
- **Engine 2 — Data Moon (DaaS / AudienceLab alternative):** Behavioural data set that reaches into the back end of Google etc. Cheaper, higher-volume, lower-quality/less targeted than LeadHunter ("scrummy data" possible). Investors get their own **sub-account**. We hold **8 white-label seats + unlimited data**; each client past 8 = **$100/mo** for unlimited data access. Two tiers: **Catalyst/Venture** = 25,000 credits/mo; **top Venture tier** = unlimited.

### Two funnels (buyers vs. leads)
- **Buyer funnel:** finds *businesses that want to buy leads* (the client's customers). Priority #1 early — "none of these leads matter unless they've got buyers." Built from LeadHunter scraping + paid opt-in.
- **Lead funnel (fulfilment):** the actual leads we/our clients deliver to those buyers. Opt-in leads are fine to resell; booked-call funnels for buyers.

### Supporting stack
- **Lead Distro:** distribution platform. We have a deal with the founder — **40% referral** when clients graduate to their own version. We plug leads in; the end buyer (roofer, financial services, etc.) buys through the platform. Dimitri owns the ops side.
- **Circle course:** the actual course wrapping the offer — teaches the investor they're the CEO of a pay-per-lead business, how the industry works, how to automate with AI, so they're autonomous after 12 months (still pay a SaaS fee to keep the software updated → recurring revenue for us). Taylor added as admin.
- **Speed-to-lead:** required on all fronts. Either Scott's white-label GHL-native system (~a few hundred $/mo, they manage implementation + monitoring, we brand it) or we build our own. Resellable either way.
- **Perspective.io/.so/.co:** 30-day trial live. Funnel builder everyone raves about; Taylor to evaluate, possibly test a funnel.
- **Will's lead-layers PDF:** one-pager. Layer 1 = LeadHunter; Layer 2 = Data Moon; plus opt-in funnels. Built on a **20% close-rate minimum** assumption. Needs Taylor's review for errors / missing copy.

### 3b — Republic ops architecture (Dmytro's GHL-first draft, ops call)
Principle: **~99% of Republic runs inside GoHighLevel** — avoid custom builds, and avoid onboarding clients into third-party systems we don't own. Each Republic client's "agency in a box" has two halves:

**B2B half — get the client BUYERS (businesses that buy leads):**
- Landing pages/funnels for the client's new agency, **one per ICP** (niche + location, e.g. "HVAC, Chicago, specific zip codes, pay-per-lead")
- Buyer pipeline (new lead → … → closed/won buyer) so progress is trackable
- **Invoicing flow:** monthly count of qualified leads delivered → auto-generate invoice → check payment → close if received / escalate if not (Stripe automations candidate)
- Reporting dashboard per buyer + rollup for the client's whole agency/business

**B2C half — get the LEADS those buyers pay for:**
- Landings/funnels/creatives per ICP to attract the actual end-leads
- **Qualification pipeline per ICP** — hard-rule automations (form fields, parameters, website checks). Must run in **minutes, not a day** — the lead has to still be warm. AI can qualify on many parameters *without* outbound calling
- Immediate delivery of qualified leads into the matching buyer's pipeline **and** the buyer's own system (e.g. Housecall Pro)
- **Routing logic:** e.g. 10 buyers match an ICP and 50 qualifying HVAC leads arrive → round-robin equal distribution across their pipelines/systems
- **Buyer active/inactive flag (hard logic):** buyer goes bankrupt or pauses → flip inactive → lead routing stops instantly
- Nice-to-have: automated buyer onboarding, contract, and payment processing

**Two service levels per buyer (a pricing lever):**
1. **Basic** — qualify against the buyer's parameters → deliver immediately on match.
2. **Premium** — qualify **and schedule an appointment** (AI outbound *or* a human setter) → deliver at the "scheduled" stage of the buyer's pipeline. Priced higher. This is exactly where the outbound-AI compliance question bites (§3c).

Dmytro is adding final points to the draft, then the plan is to **build a GHL demo and test it** before real clients land ("think 10 steps ahead so we don't rebuild").

### 3c — Speed-to-lead & outbound AI: the compliance earmark (David)
- **Inbound is fine; outbound AI voice is the risk.** GHL/Twilio gate AI outbound behind compliance (prove opt-in and get *approved*, not just attest). State law adds more — e.g. California requires the AI to disclose it's an AI, which can hurt conversions — and some verticals carry their own regulations. Building outside GHL's ecosystem lowers the threshold but trades away native integration.
- **Voice quality is a conversion risk in itself** — a "janky story-time voice" burns leads and the offer's reputation.
- Scott's package is **inbound-only** per Jerome, which sidesteps the worst of it — but the premium service level (§3b) implies outbound. **Do not let outbound AI become a silent load-bearing assumption**; earmark it now, not after landing pages, ad spend and client expectations exist downstream.
- Agreed protocol: get hands on Scott's demo and **try to break it** (David); define a **per-industry testing protocol** (voice quality + regulations per vertical) *before* choosing launch industries and going ham. Worst case: slide a human in.

### 3d — Lead Distro: adopt, replicate, or drop (open — D6)
- **For:** ready-made distribution + marketplace (per-lead payment collection built in); clients graduate to their own account once they have consistent buyers; **40% referral deal** with the founder (Call 1).
- **Against (David/Dmytro):** we've already coded round-robins in-house; Clark could rebuild the routing in hours; it appears to be a simple Vercel build we could replicate; and it means onboarding Republic clients into a third-party system that isn't ours when GHL covers ~99%.
- Not contractually locked in. Jerome sending David the login; David reviews capability parity vs a GHL-native round-robin.

### The contract / licensing model
Clients license the ability to run the ads/system on their own account for fulfilment. After the contract ends they own their version but pay a **SaaS fee** to keep it updated → recurring revenue.

## 5. Root-cause note — why the pixel is being scrapped (for the record)
1. Funnel launched clean; early conversions taught Meta a small "good" pool.
2. DQ data kept firing to the pixel (no exclusion). DQ volume outpaced qualified.
3. Tipping point → Meta optimised toward DQ lookalikes.
4. Lead-form campaigns (Joe's volume mandate, Taylor accepts partial blame) accelerated it.
5. Net today: high opt-ins, ~0 booked calls, high broke/no-show rate.
**Fix = fresh pixel + strict DQ exclusion via Zapier + no pixel on DQ pages + harder qualifying funnel.** Meta no longer needs a long warm-up with good messaging — 2–3 quality conversions and it's away.
