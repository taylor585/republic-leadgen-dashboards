What changed, and why the urgency

The pivot

Centre of gravity moves from the full AI-agency offer to the Republic lead gen offer. The agency offer is still sold, but internally only (higher margin → pricing drops, ~$15k held as a discount lever). Republic is ~90% done-for-you; sales reps can upsell buyers into the full consultancy. Emphasis goes wherever AI can carry fulfilment — that's Republic.

Contract model: clients license the system, own their version after the contract ends, and pay a SaaS fee to keep it updated — recurring revenue tail.

The cautionary tale

In March the outside team sold 18 deals in one week at a $50k price point — fulfilment was buried for two months. Republic launches at a lower price with a hungrier team. The whole spec exists so that spike never lands on one person again: Taylor must never be the bottleneck.

Go-live: sales believed to start ~Mon 20 Jul. Jerome: "seems a little early." The go/no-go gates in the Go-Live Plan decide the real date.

The three-tier lead structure

TIER 3 Ultra-high intent — the Meta siphon Top price

Our own paid Meta ads. Lead takes a specific high-value action (books a call happening within 2–3 days) → tagged as top-class resale inventory. Intent proven by action.

TIER 2 High intent — LeadHunter Core product

Prospects who declared an urgent need in their own words within the last 7 days. Not opted in, out-of-market, 0–100 scored, three internal sub-tiers to map. The ~91% match claim needs substantiation before sales quote it.

TIER 1 Low intent — Data Moon Cheapest — or cut

Behavioural tracking ("followed around the internet, graded"). Quality unproven: Jerome's cold-call spot check went nowhere; Taylor's history with this category says tire-kickers. Access received 15 Jul, evaluation underway. Fallback: sell cheap Meta leads as the budget tier instead.

Rule of thumb: Tier 1 guesses intent from behaviour; Tier 2 reads intent the prospect stated; Tier 3 has intent proven by an action in our funnel. Price follows proof. Full mechanics: Lead Tiers Research.

The model

Two supply engines

Engine 1 — LeadHunter (scraping / behavioural intent mining): finds people saying they need the service, 7-day window, 0–100 scoring, self-learning search database, no ad spend. The primary buyer-acquisition engine early.

Engine 2 — Data Moon (DaaS): cheap behavioural data, sub-account per investor. Catalyst tier 25,000 credits/mo; top tier unlimited. We hold 8 white-label seats + unlimited data; $100/mo per client after 8.

Two funnels, in order

Buyer funnel first — businesses that pay for leads. "None of these leads matter unless they've got buyers." A $20k client's first win is their first buyer conversation.

Lead funnel second — the inventory those buyers purchase. Opt-ins are fine to resell; booked-call leads are the premium product. Once a client has 1–3 steady buyers they graduate to their own Lead Distro (40% referral) or our equivalent rail.

Republic ops architecture — Dmytro's GHL-first draft

Principle: ~99% of Republic runs inside GoHighLevel. No custom builds, no onboarding clients into third-party systems we don't own.

B2B half — get the client BUYERS

B2C half — get the LEADS buyers pay for

TWO SERVICE LEVELS (pricing lever)

Basic — qualify against the buyer's parameters → deliver immediately on match.
Premium — qualify and schedule the appointment (AI outbound or human setter) → delivered at "scheduled" stage, priced higher. Premium is exactly where the compliance risk lives →

⚠ THE COMPLIANCE EARMARK (David)

Inbound AI is fine. Outbound AI voice is gated: GHL/Twilio demand proven opt-in and approval; state law adds AI-disclosure rules (e.g. California); some verticals have their own regs. Voice quality itself is a conversion risk. Do not let outbound AI become a silent load-bearing assumption — break-test Scott's demo, map compliance per industry BEFORE picking launch verticals. Worst case: a human setter slides in.

Lead Distro — adopt, replicate, or drop (D6)

For: ready-made distribution + marketplace with per-lead payment collection; clients graduate to their own account; 40% referral deal with the founder. Against: round-robins are already coded in-house, Clark can rebuild the routing in hours, it looks like a simple Vercel build, and it onboards clients into a system we don't own. Not locked in — David reviewing the login vs a GHL-native round-robin.

WHY THE PIXEL DIED (for the record)

Clean launch → early wins taught Meta a small "good" pool → DQ data kept firing to the pixel with no exclusion → DQ outpaced qualified → tipping point: Meta optimised toward DQ → lead-form volume campaigns (Joe's mandate) accelerated it.
